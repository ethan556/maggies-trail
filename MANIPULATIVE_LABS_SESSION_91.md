# Session 91 — Seven Causal Manipulative Laboratories

## Executive result

Session 91 creates and production-wires all seven laboratories recommended after Session 90. They are not isolated gallery demos. Each engine has a typed lesson schema, learner-action state, misconception-aware evaluator, semantic process events, accessible state narration, mobile-safe controls, a CML engine profile, a synchronized representation mesh, a canonical gallery sample, and at least one real K–8 lesson integration.

The release advances the app from 86 to **93 widget types** and from 80 to **87 interactive manipulative types**. Seven new flagship profiles bring the CML catalog from 46 to **53 engines**. The K–8 mesh covers **461/461 profiled manipulative steps**, generates 1,383 linked representation cards, and has no generic fallback or runtime exception.

This is an implementation-quality claim. Superiority in student learning outcomes still requires controlled learner studies using delayed retention, misconception recurrence, and transfer—not engagement or immediate correctness alone.

## Shared design contract

Every laboratory follows the same causal-mastery requirements:

1. **The learner changes mathematical state**, not an answer label.
2. **The consequence is continuously visible** in the primary model.
3. **At least three linked representations** explain the state from different mathematical viewpoints.
4. **Process events identify movement toward, away from, or across the target structure.**
5. **Feedback separates distinct misconceptions** rather than returning a generic retry.
6. **Prediction, explanation, revision, counterfactual, translation, fading, transfer, and delayed retrieval** are declared in each integrated lesson.
7. **Color semantics are stable:** sky for learner action, tangerine for target, leaf for invariant, berry for contradiction/repair, and violet for transfer.
8. **Color is never the only signal.** Labels, geometry, borders, text, and accessible narration carry the same meaning.

## 1. Line-relation construction laboratory

**Engine:** `lineRelationLab`  
**Integrated lessons:**

- Grade 4 `lines-angles / la-02-01#i1` — construct parallel lines.
- Grade 4 `lines-angles / la-02-02#i1` — construct perpendicular lines.

### Learner action

The learner rotates and translates an active line independently. The stage reports relation, smallest angle, movement evidence, equal-distance behavior, and a right-angle marker where appropriate.

### Mathematical breakthrough

The lab separates two transformations students often conflate:

- rotation changes direction and therefore angle/relation;
- translation changes position but preserves direction.

Parallelism is therefore experienced as same direction plus nonzero separation, while perpendicularity is experienced as a preserved 90-degree intersection even after translation.

## 2. Triangle-angle-sum deformation laboratory

**Engine:** `triangleAngleLab`  
**Integrated lesson:** Grade 4 `lines-angles / la-03-02#i1`.

### Learner action

The learner drags the third vertex freely. All three live angles update continuously, while the sum remains visible at 180 degrees. The task requires multiple deformations before the learner targets a specified angle.

### Mathematical breakthrough

The learner sees that individual angle measures are variables while the sum is invariant. This is stronger than a static tear-and-rearrange demonstration because the invariant survives a continuous family of triangles generated by the learner.

## 3. Continuous vertical-line scanner

**Engine:** `verticalLineScanner`  
**Integrated lesson:** Grade 8 `functions-g8 / fg-01-03#i1`.

### Learner action

The learner sweeps a vertical scanner across a continuous or discrete relation. The lab records current intersections, maximum intersections observed, number of sweeps, and the final function/not-function verdict.

### Mathematical breakthrough

The vertical-line test becomes an experiment about the one-output-per-input invariant. A verdict is not accepted until the learner gathers evidence across the relation. The engine supports linear, quadratic, circle, sideways, discrete-function, and discrete-nonfunction relation families.

## 4. Covariation scrubber

**Engine:** `covariationScrubber`  
**Integrated lessons:**

- Grade 6 `ratios-rates / rr-02-02#e1` — apples and cost.
- Grade 7 `proportional-relationships / pr-02-02#i1` — hours and earnings.
- Grade 8 `functions-g8 / fg-03-01#i1` — affine function `y = 2x + 3`.

### Learner action

One shared input controls the context sentence, highlighted table row, graph point, equation, ordered pair, and unit-rate readout simultaneously.

### Mathematical breakthrough

The learner experiences covariation rather than inspecting separate representations after the fact. The same state must agree in story, table, graph, equation, and rate. Proportional examples expose the constant of proportionality; the Grade 8 example distinguishes slope from initial value.

## 5. Sampling-design and bias laboratory

**Engine:** `samplingBiasLab`  
**Integrated lessons:**

- Grade 6 `data-distributions / dd-01-03#i1`.
- Grade 7 `sampling-and-probability / sp-01-02#i1`.
- Grade 8 `bivariate-statistics / bv-04-03#i1`.

### Learner action

The learner selects convenience, random, or stratified sampling; changes sample size; and repeatedly draws samples. The lab displays systematic bias and random variability separately, plus a deterministic repeated-estimate strip centered against the population truth.

### Mathematical breakthrough

The learner can observe two effects that conventional graph-reading questions often collapse:

- selection method moves the center of repeated estimates;
- sample size changes the spread of estimates;
- a large biased sample remains biased;
- repetition reveals both center and variability.

Changing method or size resets the draw history so comparisons remain interpretable. The CML revision history preserves earlier builds for reflection.

## 6. Attribute-first shape-family builder

**Engine:** `shapeFamilyBuilder`  
**Integrated lessons:**

- Grade 2 `shapes-shares-g2 / ssg2-01-03#i1` — pentagon family.
- Grade 3 `shapes-space / geo-01-02#i1` — square as overlapping families.
- Grade 4 `lines-angles / la-03-03#i1` — rectangle attributes.

### Learner action

The learner varies side count, right-angle count, equal-side count, and parallel-pair count. The displayed shape responds to the active rule set, and the family name is confirmed only when the defining attributes match.

### Mathematical breakthrough

Classification becomes rule construction rather than picture recognition. The builder is intentionally coordinate-free for younger grades. It prepares learners for hierarchical statements such as “a square is both a rectangle and a rhombus” without requiring coordinate-plane fluency.

## 7. Dedicated unit-iteration ruler

**Engine:** `unitRuler`  
**Integrated lessons:**

- Kindergarten `shapes-and-sorting-k / ks-03-01#i1`.
- Grade 1 `shapes-measure-g1 / smg1-03-01#i1`.
- Grade 2 `measure-money-time / mmt-01-01#i1`.

### Learner action

The learner must:

1. align zero with the object’s true start;
2. choose the requested unit size;
3. iterate units one by one;
4. choose exact, gapped, or overlapping placement;
5. compare the measured endpoint with the physical object endpoint.

The learner may overmeasure, choose a wrong unit, create gaps, create overlaps, or start from the wrong origin; each produces a distinct visible state and diagnostic response.

### Mathematical breakthrough

Measurement is modeled as unit iteration rather than reading an endpoint label. The tool directly diagnoses zero alignment, gaps, overlaps, wrong unit size, undercounting, and overcounting—precisely the concepts the reused number-line mode could not fully expose.

## Lesson integration integrity

Session 91 changes exactly **16 lesson steps across 16 lesson files**. The semantic release gate permits changes only to:

- the step body where the new action requires clearer instructions;
- the widget specification;
- prediction commitment when the original step had none;
- CML metadata for the complete causal cycle.

No variant declaration, unrelated step, lesson order, answer elsewhere, explanation elsewhere, course registration, or runtime coverage changed.

## Above-market interpretation

The public benchmark from leading interactive mathematics products is guided discovery, immediate visual consequence, polished short interactions, and adaptive support. Session 91 is designed to go further in the implemented architecture by combining:

- continuous learner-controlled mathematical state;
- synchronized representation meshes;
- explicit invariants and counterfactuals;
- strategy/process telemetry;
- preserved first attempts and revision history;
- misconception-specific feedback;
- representation translation and fading;
- transfer families and delayed retrieval.

The differentiator is not the number of widgets. It is the full evidence cycle around each mathematical action. This release still does not establish comparative efficacy; that requires student testing against current Maggie’s Trail and external benchmark conditions.
