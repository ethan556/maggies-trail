# S330 Progression Packet G9 — LESSON_PROGRESSION_AND_DUPLICATION

Scope: queue workstream `LESSON_PROGRESSION_AND_DUPLICATION` (generated by
`scripts/audit/consolidate-pending-workload-s236.mjs`, lines ~358-393), the 10 lessons assigned to
packet G9 across four courses: `sampling-and-probability` (sp, middle school), `shapes-shares-g2`
(ssg2, Grade 2), `exponents-scientific-notation` (esn, middle school), `geometry-foundations` (gf,
high school).

**Method.** The detector was reproduced verbatim (lowercase, collapse every `[-−+]?\d+(?:[.,/]\d+)*`
numeric token — sign and all — to a single `#`, collapse whitespace, cluster by identical normalized
`widget.prompt` template within one lesson, over `lesson.steps` only) as a standalone script and run
against every lesson's live JSON, both before and after edits, to confirm the queue's flagged step IDs
exactly and to prove each fix actually clears the cluster. Every lesson was read in full. Judgment: KEEP
where the collision is defensible fluency/retrieval practice, or where the colliding steps are a
deliberate paired illustration of a concept card's own stated example (a "set it up, then confirm it"
pattern used repeatedly across this session's authored content); REDESIGN where a step poses the
identical question job as an earlier step with no distinguishing content, or where a step's own body
label promises an action (interpret / match a real-world theme) its widget never actually delivers.

Result: **6 lessons with at least one REDESIGN, 4 lessons fully KEPT** (`esn-01-03`, `esn-02-02`,
`gf-03-03`, `gf-05-02` — all four resolved to legitimate deliberate pairing, zero edits).

## sp-02-01 — Are Two Groups Really Different?

Flagged: `i2`, `i3`, `k3` — all three collide with anchor `i1` on
`"group a has a mean of #. group b has a mean of #. the variability measure is #. how many
variability-units apart are the means?"`.

- `i2`'s body is **"Interpret a small gap."**, sitting right after concept `c2` (a gap under 1 unit
  means heavy overlap / not meaningfully different) — but the widget was `mode: "measure"`, i.e. it
  only asked the learner to *compute* the gap (12, 12, var 2 → 0). It never actually tested
  interpretation at all: a genuine mismatch between the step's stated job and its real one.
- `i3`'s body, **"Measure a large gap."**, does accurately describe a `measure` widget — but that
  means concept `c3`'s own claim ("several times the variability... a strong sign of a real
  difference") was never tested as an interpretive judgment anywhere in the lesson.
- `k3` ("A gap with the groups reversed," testing order/sign-invariance with a `-3` trap) has a real,
  independent misconception target — but unlike siblings `k1`/`k2`, which were both given real-world
  story wrappers, `k3` was left in the bare Group-A/B phrasing.

**Decision: REDESIGNED all three.**

- **`i2`**: mode `measure` → `judge` (the widget's other native action). New prompt: *"Two after-school
  clubs have a gap-in-units of 0.6 between their average attendance. What does this suggest?"*
  (`gapUnits: 0.6`, correct = "not meaningfully different," plus a fresh third distractor — "must have
  identical attendance every week" — distinct from the "one club has more" distractor). This now
  actually exercises the small-gap interpretation `c2` teaches.
- **`i3`**: mode `measure` → `judge`. New prompt: *"Two summer camps have a gap-in-units of 5 between
  their average enrollment. What does this suggest?"* (`gapUnits: 5`, correct = "clearly, meaningfully
  different," third distractor = "the data must be an error"). This mirrors sp-02-02's already-shipped
  `i1`(judge, small)/`i2`(judge, large) pair, closing the same gap in this lesson.
- **`k3`**: reworded to a real-world story, keeping the exact order-reversed/sign-trap pedagogy —
  *"Two choir recitals' audience counts average 22 and 40 people, with a variability measure of 6
  people..."* Verified by hand: |40−22| = 18, 18÷6 = **3**; trap `-3` is the sign-confusion distractor,
  trap `18` is the forgot-to-divide raw gap; neither equals 3.
- **Variant handling**: `k3`'s variant was `{gen: "g7-sp-gap-units", form: "spGapReverse"}`. Read in
  `src/lib/variants.ts`: this form's `reverse` branch still emits the bare `"Group A has a mean of
  ${meanA}. Group B has a mean of ${meanB}...."` template — regenerating would have silently reverted
  the fix. **Deleted** the variant field (hand-authored static widget instead). `i2`/`i3` never had
  variant fields.
- **Test-file consequence**: `src/lib/session131.distribution-compare.test.ts` hardcodes the sp course's
  total measure/judge split (it previously asserted 17 measure / 9 judge, with an existing comment
  documenting an S327 measure→judge move on `sp-02-01/ch1`). Converting `i2`/`i3` shifts two more steps
  from measure to judge, so the assertions were updated to **15 measure / 11 judge**, with a new comment
  citing this report — the same pattern the file already used for the S327 precedent.
- **Verification**: `node -e "JSON.parse(...)"` passed; `npx vitest run
  src/lib/session131.distribution-compare.test.ts` passed (7/7); `npx vitest run
  src/lib/session274.samplingProbabilityCourse.test.ts` passed (2/2); the detector-replica script shows
  zero remaining flagged steps in this lesson.

## ssg2-02-01 — Counting a Grid of Squares

- All three were accidental duplicates of the same bare compute-the-total job: `k2`'s "wider"/`k3`'s
  "taller" body labels are cosmetic only (nothing in either question actually tests wide-vs-tall), and
  `i3`'s prompt even carried an authored grammar slip ("1 rows").

**Decision: REDESIGNED all three**, as G2-appropriate representation/action changes:

- `i3` → *"A single row of 6 unit squares stretches across a windowsill. How many unit squares are
  there in all?"* (answer unchanged, 6; fixes the "1 rows" grammar as a side effect). Ties to concept

## ssg2-02-02 — Bigger Grids

Flagged: `k1`, `i2`, `i3`, `k2`, `k3` — all collide with anchor `i1` on the same generic template.

- **`k1`/`i2` are a deliberate paired illustration, not an accident.** Concept `c2` states outright:
  *"2 rows of 7 and 7 rows of 2 both give 14 squares."* `k1` (7 rows of 2 = 14, body "A tall, narrow
  grid") sits **before** `c2` and supplies exactly the first half of that concrete example; `i2` (2 rows
  of 7 = 14, body "The flipped grid") sits **after** `c2` and confirms the flip. This is a "set it up,
  then name it, then confirm it" sequence built around one specific numeric fact `c2` literally quotes —
  left **untouched**. It stays live-flagged by the structural detector (expected; disposition cannot
  suppress it) but is honestly reviewed here as legitimate.
- `i3` (a 6×6 "large square grid," body claims it illustrates "the shape of the grid can change while
  the total stays the same" per `c3`) does not actually demonstrate that: a square swapped with itself
  shows no shape change at all, so it undercuts its own concept tie-in. `k2`/`k3` ("A wide grid"/"A tall
  grid") are two more bare compute drills with only cosmetic labels and no relationship to each other or
  to `k1`/`i2`'s already-paired 7×2 fact.

**Decision: KEPT `k1`/`i2`; REDESIGNED `i3`, `k2`, `k3`.**

- `i3` gained a step-level `predict` field — *"This grid will have 9 rows of 4 squares each. If you
  flipped it to 4 rows of 9 squares instead, would the total be more, fewer, or the same?"* (outcome:
  same) — plus a reworded widget prompt, *"The grid below has 9 rows and 4 columns of unit squares.
  Count them to check your prediction."* (9×4=36, unchanged). This is a real predict-then-confirm
  transfer demand that actually tests `c3`'s claim, unlike the original square.
- `k2` → an "a row is added" framing: *"A grid starts with 3 rows of 7 unit squares each. One more row
  of 7 is added, making 4 rows in all. How many unit squares are in the grid now?"* Verified by hand:
  21+7=**28**. Traps: `21` (stale pre-addition total) and `7` (only the new row's worth).
- `k3` → the complementary "a column is added" framing: *"A grid has 8 rows of 3 unit squares each. If
  one more COLUMN (not a row) is added, how many unit squares will the grid have in all?"* Verified by
  hand: 24+8=**32**. Traps: `24` (stale total) and `27` (24+3 — adds a row's worth instead of a
  column's worth, the mirror-image mistake to `k2`'s job). Together `k2`/`k3` now test that rows and
  columns are *not* interchangeable when something is *added*, even though they *are* interchangeable
  when the whole grid is *flipped* (`i3`/`c3`) — a genuine conceptual complement, not a repeat.

Variant handling: `k2`/`k3` both carried `{gen: "grid-count", form: "read"}`, which (same generator read
as above) emits the generic template. **Deleted** both. `i3` never had a variant field (only gained a
`predict` field, which is a different mechanism). No shared generator file touched. Verification: JSON
parses; the same `session264`/`session297`/`session130` suite (13/13) and `session244` (60/60) pass;
detector-replica confirms zero remaining flags on `i3`/`k2`/`k3`, and confirms `k1`/`i2` remain
correctly, honestly live-flagged.

## gf-03-03 — Rotations as Functions

Flagged: `k2` — collides with `k1` on `"rotate (#, #) by #° counterclockwise about the origin. what is
the image's y-coordinate?"` (the 90 vs. 270 exponent — the actual rotation angle — is itself one of the
collapsed `#` tokens, hiding that the two steps drill different rotation rules).

- Concept `c1` gives all three CCW rules up front (90°, 180°, 270°). `i1` drills the 180° rule (mcq).
  `k1` drills the 90° rule numerically ("new y = old x"). `k2` drills the 270° rule numerically ("new y =
  −old x"), immediately after `i2` (mcq) tests *identifying* which rotation a mapping represents. `k1`
  and `k2` are a deliberate contrastive pair: 90° CCW `(x,y)→(−y,x)` and 270° CCW `(x,y)→(y,−x)` are the
  two rules students most often swap under pressure (both move x into the y-slot, but with opposite
  signs), and testing "what's the new y-coordinate" for both, back to back, with the identical question
  shape, is exactly the right way to isolate that one point of confusion.

**Decision: KEPT — no edit.**

## gf-05-02 — Find the Motion

Flagged: `k2` — collides with `i2` on `"a(#, #) and b(#, #) map to a′(#, #) and b′(#, #). which motion
is it?"`.

- Concept `c2` enumerates a four-case sign-pattern "fingerprint" taxonomy: only-x-flips → y-axis mirror;
  only-y-flips → x-axis mirror; both-flip → 180° rotation; swap → y=x mirror. `i2` ("Only the x
  flipped") tests the first case: `k2` ("Both signs flipped") tests the third. Both use the identical
  "which motion is it?" mcq recognition format by design — varying the format per fingerprint would
  undermine the point of testing the *same* recognition skill across *different* members of one
  enumerated classification. (The remedial for this lesson separately covers the second, only-y-flips,
  case; the fourth, swap, case is not tested by any step here — a coverage-completeness question outside
  this task's scope, not a duplication issue.)

**Decision: KEPT — no edit.**
